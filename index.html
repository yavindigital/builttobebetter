In plain language

Think of your website like a delivery truck. Every time someone loads a page, that truck has to carry your images from the server to their screen. If your images are larger than they need to be, the truck is heavier — and heavy trucks take longer to arrive.

Lighter images mean faster pages. Faster pages mean visitors wait less. And visitors who wait less are more likely to stay, read, and take action.

Why this may affect your Google rankings

Google measures how fast your pages load as part of how it evaluates websites. It calls these measurements Core Web Vitals — and page speed is one of them. Websites with heavy images can score lower on these measurements, which may affect where they appear in search results.

We are not saying that optimizing your images will automatically move you to the top of Google. Search rankings depend on many factors. What we can say is that Google has publicly stated that page experience — including speed — is a ranking signal. Carrying unnecessary image weight works against you on a measurement Google is already taking.
